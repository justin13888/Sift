# Open questions and risks

Two lists. **Open questions** are decisions not yet made; each one blocks or reshapes something specific.
**Risks** are assumptions already load-bearing in the design that may not hold.

Nothing here may be resolved by deletion. Resolving an open question means recording the answer in its
owning document and striking the entry here with a pointer to it.

| **Q-8** | ~~The fallback join when the internet message identifier is absent or duplicated~~ **Resolved:** the identifier narrows candidates and never keys a join; a stored fallback digest corroborates; ambiguity resolves to distinct messages. The three joins are all within one account, which is what let one rule serve them. R-5 stays open against the digest | — | [D-44](storage/data-model.md) |
| **Q-9** | **Maximum batch size per provider. One of four is answered.** FR-17 requires bulk operations be batched to a declared maximum. The [magnitude-capability rule](mail/provider-model.md) makes *unknown* a legal declaration that plans conservatively, so this never blocked an adapter — but each row still needs a real value and a source. **[Gmail](mail/providers/gmail.md) now declares 50, published**, and writing that row exposed something the other three will meet: the provider publishes *two* limits, a hundred calls per batch request and a thousand identifiers per label change, and the capability model has **one row** governing both envelope fetches and mutation batches. A single value has to be the minimum over the operations it covers, so mutations pay for the tighter of the two — a capability the model does not have, not a compromise an adapter made. The remaining three rows are unwritten because the adapters are (#39, #40, #41) | The throughput of every bulk operation under FR-17; no longer its correctness. Additionally: whether one row can serve two operations with different limits | [provider model](mail/provider-model.md), [gmail](mail/providers/gmail.md) |
| **Q-10** | **The reference rig specification, and the relevance corpus beside it.** The measurement *protocol* is no longer part of this — it is now stated in the owning document, so what remains open here is the machine and the data rather than the procedure. The owning document says the rig MUST be recorded once chosen and it has not been; every performance and resource figure in this set is unfalsifiable until it is. **Partly answered:** the owning document now records an [interim rig](product/reference-environment.md#interim-rig--provisional), a current developer machine marked provisional. It lets a figure name the machine that produced it. It does not answer this question: it is far from the unflattering machine required, it covers one architecture and no Linux, and it cannot pass or fail a gate. The reference rig itself is still unchosen (#69). The relevance corpus that document also requires has no interim at all (#24), and it is what [D-5](storage/search.md)'s "revisit only on measured ranking failure" needs before that condition can fire at all | Every NFR with a number in it, the P0 gates, and D-5's revisit condition | [reference environment](product/reference-environment.md) |
| **Q-11** | ~~Filter-list integrity, and whether the update endpoint should exist at all~~ **Resolved:** the endpoint does not exist. Every list ships inside the binary and changes only through the platform channels, so list content is reviewed and signed as code is, a subscription selects among bundled lists, and the list rows leave the egress table. R-11 stays open and is now permanent for lists as for code; what remains of the integrity gap is the moment a list is taken into the tree | — | [D-111](rendering/content-blocking.md), [threat model](security/threat-model.md) |
| **Q-12** | **NFR-8's and NFR-9's numbers, and the reading peak neither of them names.** NFR-8 was restated against toolkit residue when [D-2](architecture/process-model.md) reversed and is now a placeholder rather than an estimate; Linux is expected to be worse and may reopen D-2. NFR-9 inherits it, and repairing its definition against NFR-46 exposed a third state — window, filter engine and a warm body view at once, which is what reading a message costs — that no requirement budgets at all. **All three MUST be re-derived together**, not one at a time. One input it needed has since been supplied: [D-54](rendering/webview-isolation.md) fixes the number of live body views at one, so the reading peak names a determinate state rather than a quantity that varies with thread presentation. The numbers are still unmeasured | NFR-8, NFR-9, the unbudgeted reading peak, the shed-tier targets that subtract from them, and D-2's standing | [memory pressure](runtime/memory-pressure.md) |
| **Q-13** | **Whether the platform contact store is reachable under Flatpak.** [D-41](architecture/presentation-layer.md) resolves display names from the platform's own contact store; under Flatpak that runs through a portal, and whether one exists with the needed read access is unverified. Same shape as R-10 | FR-40 on Linux only | [presentation layer](architecture/presentation-layer.md), [platforms and distribution](product/platforms-and-distribution.md) |
| **Q-14** | **Where hostile image bytes are decoded.** [D-29](rendering/content-blocking.md) decodes images in the broker to classify them for the dark transform, and the broker is in the core — under [D-2](architecture/process-model.md) the single resident process holding sync state, the mutation queue, and credential material in memory. The engine's own decoders run sandboxed, out of process, and are destroyed on teardown, so as it stands the design moves attacker-controlled bytes out of the disposable half of the system and into the trusted half, where no layer backstops a decoder defect. Three answers are open, and they cost different things: decode in a sandboxed or separate process, which costs a process and partially reopens D-2; require memory-safe decoders as a stated constraint, which is cheap to write and narrows the image formats mail can carry; or accept the placement explicitly, which at least stops it reading as an oversight. **D-29 argues the transcoding question and never reached this one** | NFR-19's crash guarantee, D-29's placement, and D-2's process count | [content blocking](rendering/content-blocking.md), [threat model](security/threat-model.md) |
| **Q-15** | ~~The resolved cascade is viewport-dependent and the transform is not~~ **Resolved:** the body view is pinned to a fixed layout width and the cascade resolves media queries at that same width, so resizing the reader moves the column and never reflows the document. Preferred because it costs nothing elsewhere — recomputing on resize would put NFR-41's 30 ms onto an unbounded drag, and dropping media queries would weaken D-27's own case — and because it is already D-50's retreat for content height, so one mechanism serves both | — | [dark mode](rendering/dark-mode.md) |
| **Q-16** | ~~The licensing constraint the App Store channel implies~~ **Resolved:** the App Store channel is deferred ([D-33](product/platforms-and-distribution.md), amended), AGPL-3.0 is kept, and no contributor agreement is required now. The agreement question reopens if and when the App Store channel is reconsidered, and must be answered before the first external contribution that channel would need. Deferring was preferred to requiring an agreement for a channel the first release does not use, and to relicensing permissively, which cannot be undone. No agreement reaches work Sift does not own: that is D-112 for bundled artefacts, and Q-21, still open, for the dependency tree | — | [D-113](product/platforms-and-distribution.md) |
| **Q-17** | ~~The licence of everything Sift bundles but does not own~~ **Resolved:** each artefact is named with its terms. EasyList and EasyPrivacy are copyleft, so they ship under GPL-3.0 in the Cask and Flatpak builds and not in the App Store build, whose user can import them as custom rules; the email list and the sender-infrastructure list are written by the Sift project, which is D-37's curator; the fonts are metric-compatible and Noto faces, all under the SIL Open Font License 1.1 and shipped unmodified. The compiled rule form is treated as an adaptation and ships only where its list does; the generated stylesheet is never conveyed. What remains is Q-16, which the two Sift-written lists now fall under, and which [D-113](product/platforms-and-distribution.md) has since answered | — | [D-112](product/platforms-and-distribution.md), [content blocking](rendering/content-blocking.md), [sender origin](rendering/sender-origin.md) |
| **Q-18** | **The list endpoint's address and payload are frozen by the first release.** **This is not Q-11**, which asks whether the endpoint should exist and whether what it serves is signed. This asks what its permanence costs given [D-33](product/platforms-and-distribution.md): Sift never updates itself, so a build keeps requesting the address it shipped with for as long as it stays installed — which under Flatpak or a Cask a user never upgrades may be years. The first release therefore fixes a URL and a payload format that every later change must remain compatible with, at that address, indefinitely. Nothing has accepted that operational commitment, and it is cheap to shape now and impossible to reshape later. Open: version the address from the outset, serve a format carrying its own version, or answer Q-11 in the direction that removes the endpoint and the question with it | FR-27's update path, D-37's list updates, and whatever Q-11 decides | [privacy](security/privacy.md), [platforms and distribution](product/platforms-and-distribution.md) |

| **Q-19** | **Whether a crash-report endpoint exists at all, and who operates it.** [D-35](security/privacy.md) fixes what a report may contain and [privacy](security/privacy.md) carries a row for the endpoint in its egress table, and nothing fixes its address, its operator, its retention, or whether it ships in the first release. **This is Q-18's argument reaching a second endpoint.** That question says a build under [D-33](product/platforms-and-distribution.md) keeps calling the address it shipped with for as long as it stays installed, and scopes itself to the filter-list endpoint; the crash endpoint is permanent for exactly the same reason and is absent from the [identifier register](product/platform-baseline.md), which claims to hold what the project consumes permanently. Open: operate it, drop crash reporting from the first release and keep D-35 as the seam, or ship the report as a file the user sends themselves — which needs no endpoint and no permanence at all | D-35's upload path, and a row in the egress table that currently describes nothing | [privacy](security/privacy.md) |
| **Q-20** | **Whether a sandboxed build can disable the platform's own crash collector.** [D-35](security/privacy.md) requires Sift to disable the operating system's core dumps, on the reasoning that leaving them enabled means the no-credentials-in-dumps guarantee "holds only for the path Sift controls". [D-45](product/platform-baseline.md) requires **both** macOS channels to be sandboxed. Whether the first is possible under the second is unverified, and if it is not, D-35's guarantee fails on the channel D-33 defines as reaching everyone else. It belongs beside the P0 login-item spike and is not in it | D-35's guarantee on the App Store channel | [privacy](security/privacy.md), [platform baseline](product/platform-baseline.md) |
| **Q-21** | **The licences of the dependency tree, which is a third population Q-16 and Q-17 do not reach.** [Workspace](build/workspace.md) requires a licence gate and cannot say what it will find. Q-16 is about Sift's own copyright and a contributor agreement; Q-17 is about the filter lists, the sender-infrastructure list and the fonts. **Neither reaches the vendored crates**, which ship inside the same binary at two orders of magnitude more artefacts, and where a single copyleft crate defeats the App Store channel exactly as Q-17's copyleft list would — with the agreement fully in place. R-12 comes closest and stops at the boundary: it counts what this project builds and cannot count what it imports. Open: audit before the first release, or accept that the channel decision is being made by whatever gets vendored | D-33's App Store channel, from the direction nothing was watching | [workspace](build/workspace.md) |
| **Q-22** | **NFR-26's perceptual metric and its threshold.** [D-64](build/verification.md) requires every gate to state its metric, its threshold and how the threshold was derived, and registers this one as outstanding rather than leaving it as a sentence in a rendering document. The metric is the harder half: a pixel-difference count fails on font hinting and passes on a colour inversion, which is backwards for what the gate is for. The threshold must be calibrated against [fidelity corpus](product/reference-environment.md) pairs already agreed to render acceptably, so it describes the corpus rather than the current build — which means it is blocked on Q-10 like everything else that needs a corpus. Until both are recorded the gate reports a figure and blocks nothing | NFR-26's standing as a gate, and R-13's drifting baseline, which cannot be reasoned about without a threshold | [verification](build/verification.md) |

## Risks

Ordered by how much of the design they would invalidate. R-12 is last under that rule and first under any
other, for the reason its own row gives.

| ID | Risk |
|---|---|
| **R-1** | **Gmail restricted-scope verification is a business blocker, not a technical one.** It requires a recurring third-party security assessment or the client is capped and shows an unverified warning. Read against [D-33](product/platforms-and-distribution.md) the escape hatches collapse rather than multiply: per-user OAuth client identifiers cannot work for the App Store audience D-33 defines as everyone else, and organizational-tenants-only gives up the consumer Gmail user. What remains is **fund the assessment, or ship without Gmail on the largest channel** — a budget question. Note also that P1 builds against JMAP, so the first working client serves the smallest provider population while the largest sits behind this. **Resolve before writing the Gmail adapter.** See [credentials](security/credentials.md) |
| **R-2** | **No footprint ratchet over 14 days (NFR-12) is the hardest requirement in this set.** It is an allocator, fragmentation, and cache-discipline problem visible only in long soak tests. The [soak harness](runtime/observability.md) must exist in P0, not P4 |
| **R-3** | **"Idle" is not free even with zero work.** Fifteen live encrypted connections mean renegotiation, NAT keepalives, and re-arming timers. NFR-10 and NFR-11 may be unachievable at five accounts without dropping secondary folders to polling. See [scheduling](runtime/scheduling.md) |
| **R-4** | **The mutation intent model is where "read-only" quietly becomes expensive.** Divergent archive and delete semantics, identifiers that change on move, thread fan-out, and undo after a server-side change are a deep well. Budget it as a subsystem. See [mutations](mail/mutations.md) |
| **R-5** | **The internet message identifier is not reliably unique.** Some servers and senders duplicate it; some omit it. [D-44](storage/data-model.md) now defines the fallback, so this is mitigated rather than unaddressed — but the risk is unchanged in kind: D-44 corroborates with a digest over headers assumed to survive transit, and that assumption has no corpus behind it. Measuring it belongs with the [fidelity corpus](product/reference-environment.md) |
| **R-6** | **Two rendering engine ports multiply QA rather than adding to it.** The fidelity corpus must pass on both, and font and colour-management differences produce visible diffs that layout equivalence does not prevent. See [platforms and distribution](product/platforms-and-distribution.md) |
| **R-7** | **Dark mode for email has no correct industry answer.** The compromise is chosen deliberately in [dark mode](rendering/dark-mode.md) and will still disappoint someone |
| **R-8** | **Optimistic UI plus eventual consistency produces visible flicker.** Related to [D-38](mail/mutations.md), but distinct: even the best reconciliation policy is visible when the server disagrees often |
| **R-9** | **The full CSS cascade ([D-27](rendering/dark-mode.md)) may not fit NFR-41.** It is a style system in miniature sharing a 30 ms budget with the sanitizer and the blocker, and its fallback is a visibly worse product rather than a slower one |
| **R-10** | **Flatpak may not grant what D-14 needs.** Network-condition detection reads NetworkManager over D-Bus; the portal alternative supplies neither the metered flag nor the link class. Credential access has the same shape. See [platforms and distribution](product/platforms-and-distribution.md) |
| **R-11** | **No urgent-fix path exists.** [D-33](product/platforms-and-distribution.md) gives delivery to platform channels, so a security fix reaches users on App Review's and the distribution system's schedule, in a product whose primary adversary chooses the input |
| **R-13** | **NFR-26's gate has a baseline that drifts on somebody else's schedule.** [D-15](product/platforms-and-distribution.md) pins the Linux engine deliberately, because "version skew is the dominant Linux rendering risk". Nothing pins the macOS engine: it ships with the operating system, out of band, so a system update changes one side of a macOS-versus-Linux perceptual diff with no Sift commit and no signal other than the gate failing. Hosted runners compound it, since they are re-imaged and eventually retired, at which point every stored snapshot must be regenerated — and regeneration is exactly the operation that can absorb a real regression without anyone seeing it. This is distinct from R-6 below, which says two ports multiply QA, and from Q-10 above, which asks for the performance rig: the rendering gate has a machine of its own whose OS version is part of its baseline, and on one platform that version cannot be held still |
| **R-14** | **NFR-50's accessibility bridge is gated in P1 and has never been shown to exist.** [Webview isolation](rendering/webview-isolation.md) names five properties working against it — a separate document, a separate process, opaque per-view tokens, a non-persistent isolated store, and script disabled — and then makes the bridge normative without establishing that the tree crosses under those constraints. It is now a P0 spike. On Linux it additionally needs the accessibility bus reachable from inside the Flatpak sandbox, which is the same shape as R-10 and Q-13 — except that those are features permitted to be absent, and this is a requirement a phase gate depends on |
| **R-15** | **[D-74](storage/data-model.md)'s two files make the optimistic path two durable writes.** An intent must be durably enqueued in the journal before it is applied to the store, so the sequence that [NFR-7](architecture/ui-shell.md) budgets at 16 ms now contains a journal `fsync` that the single-file design did not. [D-85](mail/mutations.md) adds a second durable write when an intent is issued. Neither has been measured, and NFR-7 is the requirement a user feels most directly — it is the whole of what "optimistic" means to them. If the cost is real, the answers are group commit across intents or a weaker durability barrier, and the second reopens the ordering rule D-74 rests on |
| **R-16** | **[D-75](storage/encryption.md)'s two crypto implementations can disagree, and the disagreement is silent.** One portable interface over two platforms' libraries produces one format only as long as both agree about it, and a mismatch does not fail loudly — it writes a store the other platform cannot read, or worse, reads one incorrectly. [Verification](build/verification.md) requires a byte-for-byte fixture check from the first commit, which is the mitigation; the risk is that the check is the only thing standing between the design and a class of bug a single vendored implementation would not have. Related to R-6 in kind — two ports multiply QA — and worse in consequence, because this one reaches the bytes |

| **R-12** | **The bespoke-component count is the risk this documentation set's own format cannot show.** Every decision here is defended against its local alternative and every defence holds on its own page. Summed, they commit to building a spec-conformant HTML tree builder and allowlist policy ([D-26](rendering/sanitizer-invariants.md)), a CSS cascade ([D-27](rendering/dark-mode.md)), an IMAP client ([D-31](mail/providers/imap.md)), a hand-written Graph client ([D-13](mail/provider-model.md)), a timing wheel ([D-25](runtime/scheduling.md)), a page-level database encryption layer ([D-42](storage/encryption.md)), a tagging global allocator ([D-24](runtime/observability.md)), a network-conditions abstraction ([D-14](runtime/network-conditions.md)), an observed and diffed view-model layer ([D-18](architecture/presentation-layer.md)), a soak harness ([NFR-45](runtime/observability.md)), and two full native shells ([D-1](architecture/ui-shell.md)). One decision per page, each with its own counter-argument, is what makes this set trustworthy — and it is also what guarantees no page shows the total. Nothing here sizes a phase, and nothing records a team or a schedule, so the aggregate is the one commitment nobody has explicitly accepted. The three weakest by cost against argument are named on their own pages: D-27, which R-9 says may not fit its budget and whose retreat is a visibly worse product; D-1's second shell, which [D-9](product/platforms-and-distribution.md) concedes is a second full UI rather than a recompile; and D-31, whose own text records that "small subset of IMAP" has defeated better-resourced projects. **This is a delivery risk rather than a design defect, which is the only reason it sits last** |
